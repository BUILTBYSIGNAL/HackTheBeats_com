Every control on the player, region by region, with what it does, the key that does the same, and whether it needs an account. The sections after that list the keys in one place, what a MIDI controller can drive, the addresses the site answers to, and the limits of what it does.

The screen

From the top: the top bar holds the two decks, the transport and the actions. Below it the stage shows the song's code, lit as it plays, with the stage bar of editing and view buttons along its foot. Under the stage runs the arrangement strip, 32 bars wide. Under that is the deck, in four panels: Song knobs, Channels, Pads and Master. The footer carries the links to this guide, the About page and the privacy notice.

On a phone the same regions stack. The deck shows one panel at a time behind four tabs, Knobs, Mixer, Pads and Master, a Songs button joins the top bar, and the song map sits behind Map in the stage bar. Windows narrower than 860 pixels, or shorter than 540, behave the same way.

In every table, Account? says whether the control needs you to be signed in. The featured beats, the knobs, switches, channels, pads and master knobs are free for anyone; editing, saving, sharing, recording, the second deck, snapshots and MIDI need a free account. See What is free, and what needs an account.

Account and Admin

Sign in with Google is the only way in; there is no password to make. The site stores your name, your e-mail address and the songs you save. An account adds editing, saving, sharing by link, recording, video clips, the second deck with Mix and the crossfader, snapshots and MIDI, and starts with one song, User demo. Signed in, the dialog shows your name, how many songs you have, Sign out, and for the site's administrator, Admin. Signing out removes your account's songs from the browser; they stay in your account.

The Admin sheet has three tabs. Beats lists the site's beats and, for each, who can play it: Anyone makes it a featured beat, up to six and never fewer than one; Members is everyone signed in; Only me is the administrator alone. Import a strudel.cc file adds beats from a strudel.cc export, skipping patterns already present. My songs lists the administrator's own songs with Publish as beat, which adds one to the beats for the administrator alone until it is opened to others, and Update beat, which puts a song's latest code on its beat. People lists everyone with an account, when they joined and last visited, and how many songs they have; private songs are only counted. Its All shared songs view lists every song people share, with Feature and Unfeature for the community shelf, and a way to switch a song's sharing off, which closes its link for good.

Keys

The player's own list, as the help sheet shows it; none of these work while a dialog is open, the single keys are off while you are typing, and nothing happens with Alt held.

Some keys only work in one place.

On a focused knob
↑ ↓ ← → adjust it, Shift makes the step fine, PageUp and PageDown take larger steps, Home and End go to the ends, and Enter, Backspace or Delete reset it.
On a focused pad
Space or Enter presses it.
Inside the code
Alt+Enter runs the edited code and Alt+. stops the deck, the same as Ctrl+Enter and Ctrl+.. Esc leaves the code.
Esc, in order
Cancels a clip being recorded, then lets picked bars go, then lets a capture go, then leaves MIDI Learn, then leaves the gallery, then releases a focused track.

On a Mac, Ctrl reads ⌘ throughout, and the site shows it that way.

MIDI

A MIDI controller can drive the deck; the site sends nothing back. Press MIDI at the right end of the arrangement strip, or Learn in the help sheet, then click a control on screen and move or press one on your hardware. Each hardware control drives one thing: binding it again replaces what it drove before. Press MIDI again or Esc to leave Learn. See MIDI in Perform.

  • Bindable: the song's knobs and switches; every channel's M, S and fader, snapshot strips included; the six effect pads; the eight snapshot pads; the master knobs; the crossfader; Mix; and both play buttons.
  • Not bindable: Sync, Latch, Reset, ●, the capture length chips, Record and Update.
  • Messages: a control change sets a knob or fader to its value; on a button it presses above half and releases below. A note presses on note-on and releases on note-off, so a pad on your hardware holds a pad on screen.
  • Where bindings live: in this browser, by the message's number rather than the device, so the same controller on another port keeps them and another browser starts with none. Forget bindings in the help sheet clears them all.
  • Browsers: Web MIDI works in Chrome, Edge and Firefox, not Safari. Connect asks the browser for access the first time; Learn needs you to be signed in.

What is not supported

The site is built for one thing, music made by code in a browser. These are its edges.

  • No MIDI out, OSC, SuperDirt, Hydra, Csound or serial. A song that calls for them runs without them. MIDI goes one way, in.
  • No audio input. The site never asks for the microphone.
  • What the browser must have. Web Audio for sound at all; AudioWorklet for recording and snapshots; Web MIDI for a controller (Chrome, Edge and Firefox, not Safari); MediaRecorder for video clips; the Fullscreen API for Gallery. The site must be served over http or https, not opened from a file.
  • The arrangement strip edits the first 32 bars. One cycle is one bar everywhere on the site, and a paste cannot take a song past 32.
  • One runtime for both decks. The two decks share one Strudel runtime, which is why they keep one tempo and why pads like Half act on both.
  • Limits: a song's code can be up to 200,000 characters; a snapshot lasts at most 30 seconds; a recording stops itself at 20 minutes.
  • What the code reader needs. A knob's minimum, maximum and step must be plain positive numbers: slider(0, -12, 12) reads as 0 to 12. See How code becomes controls.
  • No version history. Save writes over your song; keep a version with Save as new before you need it.
  • A shared song asks first on the main site: "Run this song" or "Run and keep a copy", with "Read the code first" beside them. Its own page at /s/<uuid> plays without asking, and without editing.
  • Nothing is kept while storage is blocked. A browser that refuses storage keeps no positions, snapshots or MIDI bindings between visits.
